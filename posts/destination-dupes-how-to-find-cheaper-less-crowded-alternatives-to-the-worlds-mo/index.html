Destination Dupes: How to Find Cheaper, Less Crowded Alternatives to the World's Most Overrated Cities

2026-06-07 · Destinations · Tags: budgettravelpro, Destination, destinations

Every traveler knows the feeling: you save up for months, book that flight to Paris, and then spend your entire trip dodging selfie sticks and paying $12 for a croissant. The world's most iconic cities are undeniably beautiful, but they're also undeniably expensive—and often so packed that you spend more time in queues than actually experiencing the place. But here's the secret that savvy budget travelers have known for years: you don't need to skip these destinations entirely. You just need to find their dupes—cheaper, less crowded, and often more authentic alternatives that deliver 80% of the experience for 50% of the cost. In this guide, I'll show you how to identify these hidden gems, share some of my favorite swaps, and explain why embracing the dupe might just be the smartest travel decision you'll ever make.

Why Popular Destinations Are Overpriced (and Overrated)

It's no accident that cities like Paris, Rome, and Barcelona are on every bucket list. They have centuries of history, world-class art, and iconic landmarks. But that popularity comes with a price tag—literally. Tourist hotspots inflate prices for everything from accommodation to street food, and the sheer volume of visitors means you're often competing for space, not just deals. The economics are simple: high demand drives up costs, and the local economy becomes geared toward extracting maximum value from tourists rather than serving residents. This isn't to say these cities aren't worth visiting—they absolutely are. But if you're on a budget, you're paying a premium for the name, not necessarily for a better experience. In fact, many travelers report that the magic of these places gets diluted by the crowds, the chain stores, and the tourist-trap restaurants. The real charm often lies in the quieter, more affordable corners of the same country—or even the same region.

The Art of the Destination Dupe: How to Spot a Great Alternative

Finding a great dupe isn't just about picking a random smaller city. It requires a bit of research and a willingness to think differently. Here are the key criteria I use when scouting for a budget-friendly alternative:

  • Similar vibe, different price point: Look for cities that share the same architectural style, cultural heritage, or natural landscape as the popular destination, but without the international fame. For example, if you love the romantic canals of Venice, consider the lesser-known Bruges in Belgium or even the Dutch city of Utrecht.
  • Check the flight and train costs: Sometimes the dupe is actually easier to reach than the original. A regional airport might have budget airline connections, or a train ride from a nearby hub could be cheaper than flying into the main city.
  • Look at accommodation prices: Use booking sites to compare average nightly rates. A dupe should offer at least a 30% savings on hotels or rentals. If the difference is negligible, it's not a true dupe.
  • Read local blogs and forums: Travelers who live in or near these cities often share tips on where to go for a more authentic experience. Search for phrases like "underrated cities in [country]" or "cheaper alternative to [city]."
  • Consider the season: A dupe might be even more affordable in the shoulder season, but the key is that it's always less crowded than the original, regardless of when you go.

Once you've identified a few candidates, do a quick cost comparison for a 5-day trip: flights, accommodation, food, and attractions. You'll often find that the dupe saves you hundreds, if not thousands, of dollars—and you'll get a more relaxed, immersive experience.

Five Destination Dupes That Will Save You Big

To get you started, here are five of my favorite swaps that have consistently delivered on both savings and experience:

1. Lyon instead of Paris. France's gastronomic capital offers the same bouchon restaurants, Renaissance architecture, and riverside charm as Paris, but with a fraction of the crowds and prices. You can stay in the vibrant Presqu'île district for half the cost of a comparable Parisian hotel, and the city's famous traboules (hidden passageways) are a delight to explore. Plus, Lyon is a perfect base for day trips to the Beaujolais wine region.

2. Valencia instead of Barcelona. Barcelona's beaches and Gaudí architecture are iconic, but the city has become a victim of overtourism. Valencia, just a few hours down the coast, offers a stunning mix of futuristic architecture (the City of Arts and Sciences), a beautiful old town, and some of the best paella in Spain—all at significantly lower prices. You'll also find fewer tourists and a more laid-back vibe.

3. Chiang Mai instead of Bangkok. If you're heading to Thailand, Bangkok is a whirlwind of energy, but it's also crowded and increasingly pricey. Chiang Mai, in the mountainous north, gives you the same temple-hopping, street food, and night markets, but with a cooler climate and a fraction of the cost. You can rent a scooter for a few dollars a day, eat delicious khao soi for under $2, and stay in a boutique guesthouse for less than $20 a night.

4. Porto instead of Lisbon. Portugal's capital has exploded in popularity, driving up prices and crowds. Porto, the country's second city, offers the same colorful tiled facades, port wine cellars, and riverside views, but with a more authentic, working-class feel. The Douro Valley is right on your doorstep, and you'll find that your money goes much further—both for accommodation and dining.

5. San Sebastián instead of Barcelona (or even Madrid). If you're after a beach city with incredible food, San Sebastián in the Basque Country is a hidden gem. It's not exactly cheap, but it's still more affordable than Barcelona, and the pintxos culture is unbeatable. You get a beautiful bay, Michelin-starred restaurants (if you're splurging), and a relaxed atmosphere that's perfect for a budget-conscious foodie.

How to Make the Most of Your Dupe Destination

Once you've chosen your dupe, the key is to treat it as a destination in its own right, not just a consolation prize. Here are some tips to maximize your experience:

  • Embrace the local culture: Since there are fewer tourists, you'll have more opportunities to interact with locals. Learn a few phrases in the regional language, ask for recommendations, and be open to spontaneous adventures.
  • Seek out the unique attractions: Every dupe has its own hidden gems that the original doesn't. For example, in Lyon, don't miss the Roman theaters and the silk-weaving history; in Valencia, explore the Turia Park, a former riverbed turned into a lush green space.
  • Use the savings to upgrade your experience: The money you save on accommodation and food can be reinvested in a special experience—a cooking class, a guided tour, or a hot air balloon ride. This way, you're not just saving money; you're enhancing your trip.
  • Be flexible with your itinerary: Dupes often have less tourist infrastructure, which means you might need to plan a bit more. But that's part of the adventure. Use local transport, walk more, and let serendipity guide you.

Remember, the goal isn't to settle for less—it's to discover something even better. Many travelers who try a dupe end up preferring it to the original because it feels more real, more personal, and more memorable.

When to Splurge on the Original (and How to Do It Smartly)

Of course, there are times when you simply have to see the Eiffel Tower or the Colosseum. If you've always dreamed of visiting a specific iconic city, don't force yourself into a dupe—you'll only feel disappointed. Instead, use the dupe strategy to make the original more affordable. For example, you could stay in a cheaper suburb or a nearby town and commute in, or visit during the off-season. You could also combine a dupe with the original: spend a few days in Lyon and then take a high-speed train to Paris for a day trip. This way, you get the best of both worlds without breaking the bank. The key is to be intentional about your spending. If you know you're going to splurge on a big-ticket item like a museum pass or a fancy dinner, cut costs elsewhere—maybe by choosing a budget hostel or eating street food for most meals.

Conclusion

The world is full of incredible places that don't make the top of every travel list. By embracing the concept of destination dupes, you can stretch your budget further, avoid the crowds, and experience a more authentic side of a country. It's not about settling for second best—it's about being a smarter traveler. So next time you're planning a trip, ask yourself: is there a Lyon to my Paris, a Valencia to my Barcelona? The answer might just lead you to your new favorite place. And your wallet will thank you.
